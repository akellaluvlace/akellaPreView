III
— Chapter Three · The Doctrines

Five tenets of the quiet trade.

An old craft endures by holding to its measures. These are ours, written in the front matter of every catalogue and refused to no inquirer.

"A bookseller without principles is merely a clerk who handles paper." — Julian Thorne, Proprietor
  1. I

    On Provenance

    Every book carries the memory of its prior keepers. We accept no item whose origin we cannot trace, and refuse to obscure what we know.

    — Tenet · I
  2. II

    On Restoration

    We mend with restraint. The patina of time is a kind of authorship; to scrub it away is to silence the artifact.

    — Tenet · II
  3. III

    On Discretion

    The reading room is no theatre. Conversations there are quiet, and the names of our patrons are kept in the same drawer as our prices.

    — Tenet · III
  4. IV

    On the Reader

    A book is not a possession but a temporary trust. Custodianship requires the reader to leave it the better for having held it.

    — Tenet · IV
  5. V

    On the Mark of Time

    We prize foxing, dog-eared corners, marginal notes, and the faint imprint of a hand on a cover. These are the work of readers — the higher purpose.

    — Tenet · V

About the House

Founded on the principle that the physical artifact carries history as weightily as the text it bears. Vellum & Morocco is curated by Julian Thorne, bringing three decades of archival expertise to the acquisition and placement of significant printed matter. We specialize in early modern philosophy, classical translations, and fine bindings from the 16th to 19th centuries.
