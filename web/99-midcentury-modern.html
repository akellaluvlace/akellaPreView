The Process

I

Sketch

Drawn at 1:1, on tracing vellum, in graphite. Each piece begins with a stack of failed lines and one that holds.

Studio · January
II

Joinery

Mortise, tenon, dovetail. No fasteners hidden by stain. Each joint cut by hand and tapped together once, then disassembled, then closed.

Bench · February — April
III

Finish

Linseed, beeswax, time. Three coats minimum, four when the wood asks for it. Signed underneath, then shipped without a card.

— Provenance

Often asked.

Where is each piece made?

+

Every piece is hand-finished in our 312-square-metre atelier in Grand Rapids, Michigan. Wood is sourced from FSC-certified mills in northern Wisconsin and southern Indonesia.

How long does an order take?

+

Six to twelve weeks for in-edition pieces. Out-of-edition reissues run a season longer because the original drawings have to be re-tooled. We will tell you the exact day yours leaves the bench.

Do you ship internationally?

+

Yes — North America, EU, UK, and Japan as standard. Anywhere else by quote. Crating is included; freight forwarders are introduced rather than concealed.

Can a piece be refinished decades later?

+

That is the entire point. Send it back at our cost above $4,000 of value, our shared cost otherwise. Linseed and beeswax, never lacquer.

Is each piece numbered?

+

Underneath the seat, drawer, or top — burned in, not stickered. Edition number, year, and the initials of the maker who closed it.

— The Thorne & Low Promise [ TENET_05 ]

Send it back in fifty years.

We will refinish it under linseed and beeswax — the way it was first finished — and your grandchild will not know the difference. Repair is not a service we sell on top of the piece; it is part of the price you paid the day the cabinet left the bench.
