Six libraries that read you back.

A photo essay across nine empty rooms. Hotel mezzanines, courthouse benches, the bottom of staircases. Halfway through, the photographer stops describing them and lets the image do the work.

— Doctrine · 06

Five rules
of the page.

Pinned above the editor's desk since the founding issue. Bent twice. Broken once.

  1. I

    A piece earns its photograph.

    The image follows the writing, never the other way around. We have killed beautiful photographs to spare a thin essay; we have never killed a thin essay for a photograph.

    [ rule_01 ]
  2. II

    No advertorial. No sponsored. No silent sponsors.

    Subscribers fund the paper. The masthead carries no second column.

    [ rule_02 ]
  3. III

    Cite the marginalia.

    Every issue carries a Notes column. Half the marginalia are corrections to the previous issue; the other half are corrections to the next one.

    [ rule_03 ]
  4. IV

    Pay the contributor before the printer.

    The contributor closed the laptop. The printer closed the press. Both wait, but the contributor waits first.

    [ rule_04 ]
  5. V

    Print four issues a year. Quietly.

    A quarterly is a discipline before it is a publication. The first deadline kept is the founding act of the next issue.

    [ rule_05 ]
— Subscribers · 07

Pre-issue queries.

Asked by readers in the months between issues. Answered by the editors, in five lines or fewer.

01

Is Folio Quarterly available in print?

→

Yes. Each issue is offset-printed in a small atelier near Edinburgh, on Munken Pure Rough 120 gsm. The print run sits at 2,400. The digital edition is included with every print subscription.

02

Do you accept submissions?

→

Once a year, in the spring. The window is announced in the masthead column of the autumn issue and stays open for six weeks. Pieces are paid on acceptance, before the printer.

03

Can I order a back issue?

→

Issues 1 through 9 are out of print but available as facsimile reprints. Issues 10 onwards are stocked in original print. Both ship from the studio in glassine sleeves, by hand, on Wednesdays.

04

Is the typeface licensed?

→

Newsreader by Production Type, with custom italics drawn for the masthead. The licence covers print, web, and the occasional bookplate. Inquire if you wish to commission a similar custom cut.

05

Why so few sections?

→

A magazine is a way of choosing what not to print. We carry essays, interviews, notes, archive, poetry. Anything else is somebody else's quarterly.
