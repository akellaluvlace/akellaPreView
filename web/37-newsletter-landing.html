Inside the studio

A small room, a kettle, and far too many books.

The newsletter is written by hand, in pencil, on the back of last week's drafts. It gets a second pass with tea, a third pass with a kitchen timer, and a fourth pass after a walk. Only then does it find its way into a text editor.

I keep three notebooks: one for ideas that arrived too early, one for ideas that arrived too late, and one for the rare middle. The middle is what I send on Sunday.

There's a window above the desk that faces a brick wall. The wall is patient, and so is the work. I read each draft aloud at least twice before it ships — if a sentence stumbles in the mouth, it stumbles on the page.

The studio is small enough that the cat can reach every surface, which is, in its own way, a useful editorial constraint.

About the author

Writing against the grain of move fast and break things.

I started Slow Bytes because I was exhausted. Exhausted by the endless churn of new frameworks, the pressure to ship half-baked features, and the creeping bloat of modern applications.

Slow software isn't about writing code slowly. It's a philosophy of intentionality. It's about taking the time to design robust architectures, prioritizing user privacy over engagement metrics, and building tools that respect the user's attention.

Every week, I explore these themes through essays that draw on history, architecture, and personal experience in the tech industry. If you believe software should be crafted rather than merely assembled, you might feel at home here.

Reader questions

Five things people email about most. If yours isn't here, the reply address at the bottom of every issue actually goes to a person.

Is this a paid newsletter?

expand_more

No. The free archive is permanent, the weekly issue is free, and there is no paywall planned. A small voluntary subscription supports the writing if you'd like to, but it unlocks nothing the free list doesn't already get.

How long are the essays?

expand_more

Twelve to twenty-six minutes, with footnotes. They get longer when the subject demands it and shorter when restraint is the better answer. There's no minimum word count.

Will my email be shared, sold, or tracked?

expand_more

Never shared, never sold. The list is hosted on a small provider that doesn't track open-or-click pixels — open rates here are an honourable estimate, not a surveillance metric.

Can I republish, quote, or translate?

expand_more

Yes — under CC BY-NC. Translations welcome; please link to the original and email a copy of the translation when it's live so the archive can list it.

What if I miss a week?

expand_more

The archive holds every issue back to №001. Most readers don't read in order, and many save the longer pieces for a rainy Saturday. The newsletter is built to be read out of sequence.
