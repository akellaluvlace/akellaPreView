Five Tenets

Doctrine

A small set of operating rules. Worked out across ten years of shipping interfaces for fintech, logistics, and health. Held loosely. Reviewed often.

  1. I

    Utility before ornament.

    If a detail does not serve the task, it is not earning its place on the screen.

    — 01
  2. II

    Hierarchy through scale, not colour.

    Three sizes of type and one accent are usually enough. Decide what the eye should reach first.

    — 02
  3. III

    Whitespace is a primary material.

    A grid that breathes lets dense data sit calmly. Pack only when the user is hunting.

    — 03
  4. IV

    Components stay honest.

    A button looks like a button. A link looks like a link. A loading state announces itself without theatre.

    — 04
  5. V

    Ship, then sand.

    Get the structure right, put it in front of users, polish under daylight. Studio time alone never resolves the awkward edges.

    — 05

Common Questions

FAQ

Practical answers, kept short. For anything not covered, the studio replies inside two working days.

What kind of work do you take on?

+

End-to-end product design for tools that involve density, structure, or repeated tasks. Trading interfaces, logistics dashboards, design systems, internal admin. Marketing sites only when they share a system with a product.

How are engagements structured?

+

Two shapes. Either a fixed six-week sprint with a defined deliverable, or a recurring weekly retainer for embedded work. Both bill at the same effective rate; the choice is about cadence.

Do you collaborate with engineering?

+

Always. Final tokens and component decisions are made beside the team that will ship them. Static Figma exports without a build conversation tend to drift inside a quarter.

Where are you based and which timezones?

+

Berlin · CET. Comfortable overlapping with London, New York and Sao Paulo. Asia-Pacific clients are taken case-by-case with at least one weekly synchronous block.
