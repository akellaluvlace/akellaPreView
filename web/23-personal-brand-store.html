— II · Doctrine

A short list of things I believe about the work, kept in plain view so I can be held to them.

Five tenets, drafted on the back of a Lisbon coffee receipt in 2017 and revised every winter since. They are the spine of every piece I publish — and the first thing I cut when an essay starts feeling clever instead of clear.

Disagreement is welcome. The doctrine is mine; the work is everyone's.

Each tenet survived three quarters and one hostile reader before earning a Roman numeral. The folder of cuts is longer than the list itself.

  1. I.

    Plain language is a discipline.

    An idea you can't say without jargon is an idea you don't yet understand.

  2. II.

    Operators write better than writers operate.

    Drafts get sharper after a quarter inside the thing you're describing.

  3. III.

    A reader is not a user.

    Essays are for the person on the bus. Memos are for the person in the room.

  4. IV.

    Most strategy is taste.

    The frameworks come later. Choose what you can defend without a slide.

  5. V.

    Ship the essay.

    A finished piece in public is worth four better drafts in a folder.

A Sunday reflection on systems and stories.

— IX · Process

How a piece is made.

Four stages · 6–10 weeks
Studio · No. 02

A photograph from the working desk. The studio sits two streets from the Tagus, behind a green door.

  1. 01.
    Week 1 · 2

    Research

    Two weeks of reading, interviews, and walking the perimeter of the question. Notes go into a single folder; nothing gets thrown away yet.

  2. 02.
    Week 3 · 5

    Drafting

    A first draft, written quickly and badly on purpose. The point is to find the spine. Anything that sounds clever in week three is suspect.

  3. 03.
    Week 6 · 8

    Editing

    A pass for argument, a pass for line, a pass for cuts. Three trusted readers see it before anyone else does.

  4. 04.
    Week 9 · 10

    Shipping

    A final read on paper, a typeset proof, and the piece goes out at 9am on a Sunday. The next folder opens on Monday.

expand_more

expand_more

Newsletter subscribers see new books, courses, and limited drops 48 hours before anyone else. No social posts, no DMs — just the inbox.

Do you take guest essays or commissions?

expand_more

I take one or two outside commissions a year and almost no guest essays — the throughline matters more than the rate. If you have a specific brief in mind, send the question first and we'll see whether the answer earns a piece.

Where do book proceeds go?

expand_more

After printing and shipping, twenty per cent of the net goes to the Internet Archive and the Newcomers' Library Fund in Lisbon. The rest pays for the next book and the next twelve months of essays — that's it.
