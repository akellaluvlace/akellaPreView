Chapter Five · Heritage
— The House

A Georgian house,
kept quietly.

Number 17 was built in 1796 as a private residence for a Dublin merchant. It became boarding rooms in the 1930s, then sat shuttered for two decades. We took possession in the autumn of 2018 and spent three years on the restoration.

Every cornice, fanlight, and original floorboard has been preserved or carefully reinstated. What is new — the linens, the brass, the kitchen — has been chosen to sit easily beside what is old.

— VII · House Promise

What we promise,
quietly.

Four small commitments we make to every guest, written without the usual hospitality language.

I lock

Discretion.

No guest list shared, no arrival photographed, no name spoken in the lobby. The front desk holds your reservation under whatever initial you prefer; staff are briefed weekly on what privacy actually means in a fourteen-room house.

House rule · No. 01 arrow_forward
II verified

Quality, sourced.

The linen comes from Belfast, the soap from a Cork apothecary, the bread from a baker on Pleasants Street who delivers at six. We name our suppliers because we'd want to know, and because they're easier to find than to hide.

House rule · No. 02 arrow_forward
Most requested
III spa

Quietude, kept.

No piped music, no ringing phones at reception, no hum from the bar past eleven. Sash windows close on the courtyard side; the corridor carpet is twelve millimetres thick. The loudest thing you'll hear most evenings is the kettle in your room.

House rule · No. 03 arrow_forward
IV room_service

Care, attentive.

A turn-down at nine, a small note about tomorrow's weather, a handwritten card on the second night. Three of our staff have been here since opening; the others trained under them. Most of what looks like luxury is, on inspection, just paying attention.

House rule · No. 04 arrow_forward
