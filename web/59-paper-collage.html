Why We Need Messy Webs

In a world of perfect grids, the human touch is lost. We crave the tear, the smudge, the error. The wrinkled corner of a notebook page, the coffee ring on a manuscript, the line crossed out and rewritten beside it. These accidents are not failures. They are the writer's hand making contact with the paper.

Let's bring back the feeling of holding something real, even through a glass screen. Sites that breathe. Layouts that lean a little. Margins that wander, the way a good letter does when the writer can't quite hide their hurry. Pixels can fray. Edges can curl. The web should remember that it was made by people, not by templates.

A Layered Method.

Pick a stack of seven photos. Tear five of them along the long edge. Don't read the corners — let them argue. The composition is the cut, not the layout.

  • I — start with sepia.
  • II — argue with grayscale.
  • III — slip a colour scrap underneath.
  • IV — leave one corner curled.
— a method, not a rule.

Margin Notes (FAQ)

How often does a new issue land?

+

A loose monthly rhythm — usually mid-month, sometimes late. The Scrapbook is closer to a fanzine than a newsletter; if a fragment isn't ready, it waits.

Is everything hand-cut, or just the look?

+

The look. Most of the photos are mine, scanned from contact sheets; the typewriter strips are real ribbon. The page glue, sadly, is CSS.

Can I submit a fragment of my own?

+

Yes — short prose, drawings, or photographs. Email the editor with two paragraphs and a single image. We answer slowly, but we answer.

Do you ship a physical copy?

+

Twice a year, in a small risograph run. Subscribers on the wood-pulp tier get one in the post — paper that smells like paper.

Will the archive stay free?

+

Always. The web should remember things for free. Subscriptions only buy you the print run and the warm feeling of supporting a small operation.

Here, pixels are torn, images are taped, and nothing is perfectly aligned. It's a digital homage to the physical journals I've kept for years.
