How I Work

01

Decision over deck

A one-pager that names the call, the trade-off and the date beats a 30-slide explainer every time.

02

Sit with the user

I keep one weekly hour on the calendar for an unscripted call with a customer. The roadmap follows that hour.

03

Write the launch first

If I cannot draft the launch note before kickoff, the brief is not ready. The note is the spec, doubled.

04

Keep the team boring

Predictable cadences, owned surfaces, calm reviews. Drama is a tax on shipping.

FAQ

Why are you looking? ›
I am winding down a long, satisfying chapter at Acme. I want to step into an earlier-stage company where the next product call lives one room away from the customer rather than four meetings away.
What makes you a fit for early-stage? ›
I have shipped without a brand to lean on, written my own pricing pages, fielded my own support DMs and turned a four-person product org into a fifteen-person one without losing the throughline. I do not need a finished playbook.
Will you relocate? ›
Stockholm is home. I will travel up to a week a month and overlap deeply with EU and East-Coast US hours. Permanent relocation is off the table for the next two years.
What is your notice period? ›
Three months on paper, often negotiable to eight weeks. I prefer a clean handover over a fast exit.
Are you taking advisory roles? ›
Yes — at most two at any time, both seed-to-Series-A B2B SaaS, with monthly cadence. I do not take advisory roles in companies competing with current full-time employers.
Where can I see your writing? ›
A short essay archive lives at jonas.lindqvist.se/notes. The latest pieces are on launch reviews, pricing windows and the social tax of cross-functional rituals.
