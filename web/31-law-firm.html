— Standard · 05

Five principles
of counsel.

Adopted by the partnership at founding. Re-affirmed each January at the partners' retreat. Bent twice, broken never.

Read the engagement letter →
  1. I

    The client tells the story.

    We listen until the question is fully formed before we open the brief. The first hour is always free, and always spent listening.

    [ canon_01 ]
  2. II

    A flat fee where one is fair.

    For matters with a known shape — a will, an LLC formation, a closing — we quote a flat fee. The hourly meter goes off the moment we agree.

    [ canon_02 ]
  3. III

    The partner of record reads every brief.

    No filing leaves the firm without a partner's signature beside the associate's. The cover page is two names, every time.

    [ canon_03 ]
  4. IV

    Settle when settling is right; try when it is not.

    A trial costs the firm time and the client a great deal more. We recommend trial when nothing else will close the matter — and we recommend settlement when it will.

    [ canon_04 ]
  5. V

    Keep the door open after the file closes.

    A first matter is half the relationship. The second one — five years later — is the test, and we open the file the same week.

    [ canon_05 ]
— Pre-engagement · 06

Questions clients ask first.

01

What does an initial consultation look like?

A 60-minute conversation, in person or by telephone, free of charge. We listen, take notes, and at the end either propose an engagement or refer you elsewhere. No filing, no charge, no obligation.

02

How are fees structured?

Flat fee for predictable work — wills, formations, closings. Hourly for litigation and transactional work with unknown scope. Engagement letters quote both options whenever the matter permits.

03

Do you handle matters outside the state?

Three of our partners are admitted in neighbouring jurisdictions. For matters further afield we co-counsel with one of seventeen firms in our reciprocal network — the relationship is yours, the local hands are theirs.

04

Will my matter be staffed by an associate?

Every matter has a named partner of record who reads every filing. Associates draft and prepare; the partner reviews and signs. The cover page always carries two names.

05

What happens if the matter ends in trial?

Roughly one engagement in twenty reaches a courtroom. When it does, the partner of record argues the case. Trial preparation begins six months before the trial date and includes one mock examination.
